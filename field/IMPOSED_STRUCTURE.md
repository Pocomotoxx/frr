# Imposed Structure vs. Source Structure

A reasoner has structural gravity: given fragmented, unordered, or merely adjacent
facts, it will reach for a causal chain, a timeline, a hierarchy of importance, and
a narrative with a beginning and an end. This is the same gravity FRR warns about
when it says a shared silhouette is not a shared mechanism and predictive closure
is not causal power — seen now at the moment a conclusion is formed.

## The distinction

Before asserting a relation, mark where it lives:

- **CAUSAL** — "A because of B". Is the causation in the source, or is it inferred
  from adjacency? Sequence is not cause; correlation is not cause.
- **TEMPORAL** — "A before B". Did the source order the events, or did the reasoner
  order them?
- **HIERARCHY** — "A matters more than B". Is the priority stated, or assigned?
- **NARRATIVE** — "this is the story of…". Is the arc in the material, or supplied?

Four adjacent facts —

```
Revenue fell.  A new strategy was introduced.  A reorganization followed.
Employees felt uncertain.
```

— do not contain the chain `revenue → strategy → reorg → uncertainty`. A reasoner
supplies it. That supply may be correct, but it is *imposed* until a source carries
it.

## The discipline

Preserve productive ambiguity until a distinction affects a consequence. Do not let
the fluency of a causal story stand in for its evidence. When a conclusion adds
structure, the reasoner must be able to say, for each added relation, whether it is
sourced or imposed — and if imposed, say so rather than smuggle it.

## The checkable form

When a conclusion carries weight, this discipline becomes a record: the
**Closure Restraint** pass (`passes/CLOSURE_RESTRAINT.md`) binds the conclusion to
its licensing burden and requires the `imposed_structure` it added to name its
`provenance`. The field module is the reasoning; the record is where it is pinned.
