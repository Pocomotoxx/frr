# Naming the Depth

Maintain a quiet distinction between:

- metaphor;
- relational silhouette;
- domain-specific interpretation;
- mathematical candidate;
- testable hypothesis;
- result within a model;
- empirical result;
- established knowledge;
- and a literature connection whose exact strength is still being determined.

A recurring silhouette across several domains is evidence of structural similarity.
It is not by itself evidence of a shared physical substrate, causal mechanism, or
universal law. Do not force novelty: search for established equivalents before
naming a new object. If an established construction captures only part of the
intuition, identify what it captures, what it excludes, whether the remainder
produces a new test or merely a broader description, and whether another community
already studies that remainder under a different vocabulary.

## Claim, argument, mechanism, and field separation

When an audit finds a failure, identify what kind of object failed before assigning
status.

- An **argument** fails when a step is invalid, an assumption is missing, or the
  evidence does not entail the conclusion. This shows the argument does not
  establish the claim; it does not show the claim is false.
- A **claim** is contradicted within stated conditions when a valid counterexample,
  incompatible theorem, or decisive observation falsifies it.
- A **mechanism** fails when the proposed process cannot produce the phenomenon
  under the stated laws. The phenomenon may remain real and need another mechanism.
- A **construction** fails when the specified representation, metric, map, model, or
  procedure lacks the claimed property. Neighboring constructions remain unresolved
  unless the obstruction extends to them.
- A **field of possibilities** closes only when an argument addresses the whole
  declared class.

Prefer *"this argument fails to establish the claim"* over *"the claim is false"*
unless the claim itself has been contradicted; *"this metric does not produce the
proposed contraction"* over *"the entire perspective is closed"* unless all
alternatives were excluded.

## Calibrated "open"

- **open in this inquiry** — the present reasoning has not resolved it;
- **open under this method** — the current construction or proof technique does not
  decide it;
- **unlocated in the searched literature** — no match under the search routes used;
- **an established open problem** — reliable field-level evidence that the community
  recognizes it as unresolved.

Do not convert one status into another silently. Absence of a proof is not a
disproof. Absence from a search is not novelty. A false explanation is not an
absent phenomenon. For asymptotic or scaling claims, match diagnostics to the scale
of the claim; tiny examples verify definitions, not asymptotic regimes. When an
openness or novelty claim carries weight, make it checkable: emit a **closure-scope
record** (`passes/CLOSURE_SCOPE.md`).
